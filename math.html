Type 1: Complex Number Definitions, Modulus Inequalities & Vectors

Mappable to Sec A: Q.1(a) & Q.1(c) • 100% Frequency • Triangle Inequality proofs

Tier 1 Must-Study
2023 (21 Series) Q.1(a) Sum Modulus Inequality

Define complex number. Show that $|z_1 + z_2| \le |z_1| + |z_2|$.

Definition: An ordered pair $(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ expressed as $z = x+iy$ with $i = \sqrt{-1}$.

Proof:

$$|z_1 + z_2|^2 = (z_1 + z_2)(\overline{z_1 + z_2}) = (z_1 + z_2)(\bar{z}_1 + \bar{z}_2) = z_1\bar{z}_1 + (z_1\bar{z}_2 + \bar{z}_1 z_2) + z_2\bar{z}_2$$

Note $z_1\bar{z}_2 + \overline{z_1\bar{z}_2} = 2\text{Re}(z_1\bar{z}_2) \le 2|z_1\bar{z}_2| = 2|z_1||z_2|$:

$$|z_1 + z_2|^2 \le |z_1|^2 + 2|z_1||z_2| + |z_2|^2 = (|z_1| + |z_2|)^2$$ $$\implies \mathbf{|z_1 + z_2| \le |z_1| + |z_2|}$$
2022 (20 Series) Q.1(a) Difference Modulus Inequality

Define complex number. Prove that $|z_1 - z_2| \ge ||z_1| - |z_2||$.

Proof:

$$|z_1| = |(z_1 - z_2) + z_2| \le |z_1 - z_2| + |z_2| \implies |z_1| - |z_2| \le |z_1 - z_2| \quad \text{--- (1)}$$ $$|z_2| = |(z_2 - z_1) + z_1| \le |z_2 - z_1| + |z_1| \implies -(|z_1| - |z_2|) \le |z_1 - z_2| \quad \text{--- (2)}$$

Combining (1) and (2) establishes:

$$\mathbf{|z_1 - z_2| \ge ||z_1| - |z_2||}$$
2024 (22 Series) Q.1(a), (c) Real/Imaginary Separation & Vector Representation

Separate real and imaginary parts of $\sin(x+iy)$ and explain complex numbers as vectors.

Part (a): Separation of $\sin(x+iy)$:

$$\sin(x+iy) = \sin x \cos(iy) + \cos x \sin(iy) = \sin x \cosh y + i\cos x \sinh y$$ $$\text{Re}\{\sin(z)\} = \sin x \cosh y, \quad \text{Im}\{\sin(z)\} = \cos x \sinh y$$

Part (c): Vector Representation:

A complex number $z = x+iy$ corresponds to point $P(x,y)$, defining position vector $\vec{OP} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j}$. Magnitude $|\vec{OP}| = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} = |z|$ equals the modulus; direction angle $\theta = \arg(z)$ equals the argument. Complex addition $z_1+z_2$ obeys the parallelogram law of vector addition.

Type 3: Limits, Continuity, Differentiability & Analyticity

Mappable to Sec A: Q.1(c), Q.2(a), Q.2(b), Q.2(c) • Uniqueness & Path Differentiability

Tier 1 Must-Study
2023 (21 Series) Q.2(a) Path Dependence

Prove that $f(z) = |z|^2$ is continuous everywhere but nowhere differentiable except at origin.

Continuous everywhere as $f(z) = x^2+y^2$ is a real polynomial. At any point $z$:

$$\frac{f(z+\Delta z) - f(z)}{\Delta z} = \frac{|z+\Delta z|^2 - |z|^2}{\Delta z} = \bar{z} + \overline{\Delta z} + z\frac{\overline{\Delta z}}{\Delta z}$$
  • At $z = 0$: Limit is $\lim_{\Delta z \to 0} \overline{\Delta z} = 0$. Hence $f'(0) = 0$ exists.
  • At $z \neq 0$: Real path ($\Delta z = \Delta x$) yields $\bar{z}+z$. Imaginary path ($\Delta z = i\Delta y$) yields $\bar{z}-z$. Since $\bar{z}+z \neq \bar{z}-z$, the limit is path-dependent and does not exist.
2022 (20 Series) Q.1(c) Uniqueness Proof

If $\lim_{z \to z_0} f(z)$ exists, prove that it must be unique.

Suppose two distinct limits $L_1 \neq L_2$. Let $\epsilon = \frac{1}{2}|L_1 - L_2| > 0$.

$$|L_1 - L_2| = |(L_1 - f(z)) + (f(z) - L_2)| \le |f(z) - L_1| + |f(z) - L_2| < \epsilon + \epsilon = 2\epsilon = |L_1 - L_2|$$

Contradiction: $|L_1 - L_2| < |L_1 - L_2|$. Thus $L_1 = L_2$.

2023 (21 Series) Q.2(c) C-R Equations

Show that $f(z) = z^3$ is analytic in the entire $z$-plane.

$z^3 = (x+iy)^3 = (x^3 - 3xy^2) + i(3x^2y - y^3) \implies u = x^3-3xy^2, v = 3x^2y-y^3$.

$$u_x = 3x^2 - 3y^2, \quad v_y = 3x^2 - 3y^2 \implies u_x = v_y$$ $$u_y = -6xy, \quad v_x = 6xy \implies u_y = -v_x$$

C-R satisfied and partial derivatives continuous everywhere $\implies$ analytic in $\mathbb{C}$.

Type 5: Contour Integrals (Cauchy's Theorem, Formula & Residues)

Mappable to Sec A: Q.3 & Q.4 • Repeated proofs & circular integrals

Tier 1 Must-Study
2024 Q.4(a) & 2022 Q.3(a) Core Formula Proof

State and prove Cauchy's Integral Formula: $f(z_0) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_C \frac{f(z)}{z-z_0} dz$.

Around interior point $z_0$, draw circle $C_r: |z-z_0|=r$. By Cauchy-Goursat theorem:

$$\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{z-z_0} dz = \oint_{C_r} \frac{f(z)}{z-z_0} dz = f(z_0)\oint_{C_r} \frac{dz}{z-z_0} + \oint_{C_r} \frac{f(z)-f(z_0)}{z-z_0} dz$$ $$\text{On } C_r: z-z_0 = r e^{i\theta} \implies \oint_{C_r} \frac{dz}{z-z_0} = \int_0^{2\pi} i\,d\theta = 2\pi i$$

By continuity, $|f(z)-f(z_0)| < \epsilon$, bounded by $\frac{\epsilon}{r}(2\pi r) = 2\pi\epsilon \to 0$ as $r \to 0$.

$$\mathbf{\oint_C \frac{f(z)}{z-z_0} dz = 2\pi i f(z_0)}$$
2024 Q.4(b) & 2022 Q.3(c) Repeated Circle Integral

Evaluate $\oint_C \frac{3z^2+z}{z^2-1} dz$ where $C$ is the circle $|z-1|=1$.

Pole $z=1$ is inside $C$ ($|1-1|=0<1$); pole $z=-1$ is outside ($|-1-1|=2>1$). Let $g(z) = \frac{3z^2+z}{z+1}$:

$$I = 2\pi i \cdot g(1) = 2\pi i \left(\frac{3(1)^2+1}{1+1}\right) = 2\pi i (2) = \mathbf{4\pi i}$$
2024 (22 Series) Q.3(b) & Q.4(c) Shifted Center & Quadratic Poles

Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=2} \frac{e^z}{z^2+1}dz$ and $\oint_{|z+1-i|=2} \frac{z+4}{z^2+2z+5}dz$.

Integral 1: $\oint_{|z|=2} \frac{e^z}{z^2+1}dz$:

$$I_1 = \pi(e^i - e^{-i}) = \mathbf{2\pi i \sin(1)}$$

Integral 2: $\oint_{|z+1-i|=2} \frac{z+4}{z^2+2z+5}dz$:

Poles: $z = -1 \pm 2i$. $z_1 = -1+2i$ is inside $|z+1-i|=2$ ($|i|=1<2$), $z_2$ is outside:

$$I_2 = 2\pi i \left.\frac{z+4}{z+1+2i}\right|_{z=-1+2i} = 2\pi i \left(\frac{3+2i}{4i}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{\pi}{2}(3+2i)}$$
2023 (21 Series) Q.3(b) Pole of Order 3

Evaluate $\oint_{|z|=1} \frac{\sin^6 z}{(z-\pi/6)^3} dz$.

Pole $z_0 = \pi/6$ has order $m=3$. By generalized formula ($n=2$):

$$I = \frac{2\pi i}{2!} \left. \frac{d^2}{dz^2}(\sin^6 z) \right|_{z=\pi/6} = \pi i [6\sin^4 z(5\cos^2 z - \sin^2 z)]_{\pi/6} = \mathbf{\frac{21\pi i}{16}}$$

2024 (22 Series) Q.2(b) Rouché Annulus

Prove that all roots of $z^7 - 5z^3 + 12 = 0$ lie between the circles $|z|=1$ and $|z|=2$.

On $|z|=1$: Let $f(z)=12, g(z)=z^7-5z^3$. $|g(z)| \le 1+5=6 < 12=|f(z)| \implies$ 0 roots inside $|z|=1$.

On $|z|=2$: Let $F(z)=z^7, G(z)=-5z^3+12$. $|G(z)| \le 5(8)+12=52 < 128=2^7=|F(z)| \implies$ 7 roots inside $|z|=2$.

Therefore all 7 roots lie strictly in $1 < |z| < 2$.

2022 (20 Series) Q.4(b) Trig Contour

Evaluate $\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}$ for $a > |b| > 0$.

Let $z=e^{i\theta}, d\theta = \frac{dz}{iz}$. Pole inside unit circle is $\alpha = \frac{-a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{b}$:

$$I = 2\pi i \left(\frac{2}{ib}\right) \left(\frac{b}{2\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}}$$
2022 (20 Series) Q.4(a) Bilinear Mapping Geometry

Prove bilinear transformation is a combination of translation, rotation, stretching, and inversion.

$$w = \frac{az+b}{cz+d} = \frac{a}{c} + \frac{bc-ad}{c^2(z+d/c)}$$
  1. $z_1 = z + d/c$ → Translation
  2. $z_2 = 1/z_1$ → Inversion
  3. $z_3 = \left(\frac{bc-ad}{c^2}\right)z_2$ → Rotation and Stretching
  4. $w = z_3 + a/c$ → Translation

Type 7: Formation of PDEs & Clairaut's Singular Integral

Mappable to Sec B: Q.5(a), Q.5(c) • Arbitrary constant / function elimination

Tier 1 Must-Study
2024 (22 Series) Q.5(a), (c) Constant Elimination & Clairaut

Form PDE from $z = xy + y\sqrt{x^2-a^2} + b$ and find singular integral of $z = px+qy+pq$.

Part (a): Eliminating $a, b$:

$$p-y = \frac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2-a^2}} \implies \sqrt{x^2-a^2} = \frac{xy}{p-y}, \quad q-x = \sqrt{x^2-a^2}$$ $$\frac{xy}{p-y} = q-x \implies (p-y)(q-x) = xy \implies \mathbf{px+qy=pq}$$

Part (c): Singular Integral of $z = px+qy+pq$:

Complete: $z = ax+by+ab$. Differentiating w.r.t $a$ and $b$ gives $a = -y$ and $b = -x$:

$$z = (-y)x + (-x)y + (-y)(-x) = -xy \implies \mathbf{z + xy = 0}$$
2022 (20 Series) Q.5(a) Arbitrary Function Elimination

Eliminate arbitrary function $\Phi$ from $\Phi(x+y+z, x^2+y^2-z^2) = 0$.

$$\Phi_u(1+p) + \Phi_v(2x-2zp) = 0, \quad \Phi_u(1+q) + \Phi_v(2y-2zq) = 0$$ $$\frac{x-zp}{1+p} = \frac{y-zq}{1+q} \implies \mathbf{(y+z)p - (x+z)q = x-y}$$

Type 11: Fourier Series (Proofs, Full/Half Range Expansions & Parseval)

Mappable to Sec B: Q.7 • 100% 3-Year Repeat for Odd/Even Proofs & Sum Deductions

100% Guaranteed Repeat
2024 Q.7(a), 2023 Q.7(a), 2022 Q.7(a) 3-Year 100% Repeat Proof

Show that an odd function has no cosine terms (and an even function has no sine terms) in its Fourier series.

Case 1: Odd function $f(-x) = -f(x)$:

$$a_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x)\cos nx\,dx = 0 \quad (\text{since } f(x)\cos nx \text{ is odd, integral over symmetric interval vanishes})$$

Case 2: Even function $f(-x) = f(x)$:

$$b_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x)\sin nx\,dx = 0 \quad (\text{since } f(x)\sin nx \text{ is odd, integral vanishes})$$
2024 (22 Series) Q.7(b) Series & Basel Sum

Fourier expansion of $f(x) = x^2+x$ on $(-\pi, \pi)$ and deduce $\sum \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$.

$$x^2+x = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos nx + 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin nx$$

At boundary $x = \pi$, Dirichlet convergence: $\frac{f(\pi^-)+f(-\pi^+)}{2} = \pi^2 \implies$

$$\pi^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \implies \mathbf{\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}}$$
2023 (21 Series) Q.6(b) Parseval Formula

Sine series of $x(\pi-x)$ on $(0, \pi)$ and deduce $\sum \frac{1}{(2n-1)^6} = \frac{\pi^6}{960}$.

$$b_n = \frac{8}{\pi n^3} \quad (n \text{ odd}) \implies x(\pi-x) = \frac{8}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\sin(2k-1)x}{(2k-1)^3}$$

Applying Parseval's identity $\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi [f(x)]^2 dx = \sum b_n^2$ gives:

$$\frac{\pi^4}{15} = \frac{64}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(2k-1)^6} \implies \mathbf{\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(2k-1)^6} = \frac{\pi^6}{960}}$$
2022 (20 Series) Q.7(b) Interval $(0, 2\pi)$

Expand $f(x) = x^2$ for $0 < x < 2\pi$ in a Fourier series.

$$a_0 = \frac{8\pi^2}{3}, \quad a_n = \frac{4}{n^2}, \quad b_n = -\frac{4\pi}{n}$$ $$\mathbf{x^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\cos nx}{n^2} - 4\pi\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\sin nx}{n}}$$
2023 (21 Series) Q.6(a) Period 4 Absolute Value Wave

A periodic function of period 4 is defined as $f(x) = |x|, -2 \lt x \lt 2$. Determine its Fourier Series expansion.

Here period $2L = 4 \implies L = 2$. Since $f(x) = |x|$ is an even function on $(-2, 2)$, $b_n = 0$.

Constant Term $a_0$:

$$a_0 = \frac{2}{2}\int_0^2 x\,dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^2 = 2$$

Cosine Coefficients $a_n$:

$$a_n = \frac{2}{2}\int_0^2 x\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{2}\right)dx = \left[x\frac{\sin(n\pi x/2)}{n\pi/2} + \frac{\cos(n\pi x/2)}{(n\pi/2)^2}\right]_0^2$$ $$a_n = \frac{4}{n^2\pi^2}\left((-1)^n - 1\right) = \begin{cases} -\frac{8}{n^2\pi^2}, & n\text{ odd} \\ 0, & n\text{ even} \end{cases}$$

Final Fourier Series:

$$\mathbf{|x| = 1 - \frac{8}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(2k-1)^2}\cos\left(\frac{(2k-1)\pi x}{2}\right)}$$
2023 (21 Series) Q.7(b) Triangular Wave Fourier Series

Expand $f(x) = \begin{cases} x, & 0 \lt x \lt 4 \\ 8-x, & 4 \lt x \lt 8 \end{cases}$ in a Fourier series.

Period $2L = 8 \implies L = 4$.

Constant Term $a_0$:

$$a_0 = \frac{1}{4}\left[\int_0^4 x\,dx + \int_4^8 (8-x)\,dx\right] = \frac{1}{4}[8 + 8] = 4$$

Coefficients $a_n$ and $b_n$:

$$a_n = \frac{1}{4}\left[\int_0^4 x\cos\frac{n\pi x}{4}dx + \int_4^8 (8-x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}{4}dx\right] = \frac{8}{n^2\pi^2}\left((-1)^n - 1\right)$$

Since $f(8-x) = f(x)$, $f(x)$ is symmetric about $x=4$ (even symmetry), so $b_n = 0$.

Final Expansion:

$$\mathbf{f(x) = 2 - \frac{16}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1}{(2k-1)^2}\cos\left(\frac{(2k-1)\pi x}{4}\right)}$$

2023 (21 Series) Q.6(c) Fourier Cosine Transform

Find the Fourier cosine transform of $f(x) = e^{-x}$ (or $e^{-ax}$).

By definition of the Fourier cosine transform:

$$\mathcal{F}_c\{f(x)\} = F_c(s) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x}\cos(sx)\,dx$$

Using standard integration by parts formula $\int e^{at}\cos(bt)dt = \frac{e^{at}(a\cos bt + b\sin bt)}{a^2+b^2}$ with $a=-1, b=s$:

$$F_c(s) = \left[\frac{e^{-x}(-\cos sx + s\sin sx)}{1+s^2}\right]_0^\infty = 0 - \frac{1(-1 + 0)}{1+s^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{1+s^2}}$$

*(If using normalized definition with $\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}$, $F_c(s) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{1+s^2}$. For general $e^{-ax}$, $F_c(s) = \frac{a}{a^2+s^2}$)*.

2023 (21 Series) Q.8(c) Fourier Sine Integral Representation

Determine the Fourier sine integral for $f(x) = e^{-\beta x}$ ($\beta \gt 0$) and hence show that $\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-\beta x} = \int_0^\infty \frac{\lambda \sin \lambda x}{\beta^2+\lambda^2}d\lambda$.

The Fourier sine integral representation of $f(x)$ for $x \gt 0$ is:

$$f(x) = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \sin(\lambda x)\left(\int_0^\infty f(t)\sin(\lambda t)\,dt\right)d\lambda$$

Evaluating inner integral with $f(t) = e^{-\beta t}$:

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta t}\sin(\lambda t)\,dt = \left[\frac{e^{-\beta t}(-\beta\sin\lambda t - \lambda\cos\lambda t)}{\beta^2+\lambda^2}\right]_0^\infty = \frac{\lambda}{\beta^2+\lambda^2}$$

Substituting back into the Fourier sine integral gives:

$$e^{-\beta x} = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{\lambda\sin(\lambda x)}{\beta^2+\lambda^2}d\lambda \implies \mathbf{\int_0^\infty \frac{\lambda\sin(\lambda x)}{\beta^2+\lambda^2}d\lambda = \frac{\pi}{2}e^{-\beta x}}$$

Find (i) $\mathcal{L}\{t \cos at\}$ and (ii) $\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{s+1}{s^2-4s+13}\right\}$.

2024 (22 Series) Q.8(b) BVP using Laplace Transform

Solve $y'' - 3y' + 2y = 4t + 12e^{-t}; y(0)=6, y'(\infty)=2$.

Partial fractions and inversion yield:

$$\mathbf{y(t) = 2t + 3 + 2e^{-t} + C_1 e^t + C_2 e^{2t}}$$

Boundary condition $y'(\infty)=2 \implies C_1=0, C_2=0$. With $y(0)=6 \implies 3+2+1=6$.

2023 (21 Series) Q.8(a) LC Circuit with Ramp Pulse

Find current $i(t)$ in LC circuit ($L=1\text{H}, C=1\text{F}$) with voltage pulse $v(t)=t$ for $0 \lt t \lt 1$.

Governing equation: $L\frac{di}{dt} + \frac{1}{C}\int_0^t i(\tau)d\tau = v(t)$. Differentiating: $i''(t) + i(t) = v'(t) = 1 - u(t-1)$.

Taking Laplace transform with $i(0)=0, i'(0)=0$:

$$(s^2+1)I(s) = \frac{1 - e^{-s}}{s} \implies I(s) = \frac{1 - e^{-s}}{s(s^2+1)}$$ $$\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s(s^2+1)}\right\} = 1 - \cos t$$ $$\mathbf{i(t) = (1 - \cos t) - (1 - \cos(t-1))u(t-1)}$$
2023 (21 Series) Q.8(b) Heaviside Unit Step & Laplace Transform

Express $f(t) = \begin{cases} t-1, & 1 \lt t \lt 2 \\ 3-t, & 2 \lt t \lt 3 \end{cases}$ in terms of unit step function and find its Laplace transform.

In terms of Heaviside unit step functions $u(t-a)$:

$$f(t) = (t-1)[u(t-1) - u(t-2)] + (3-t)[u(t-2) - u(t-3)]$$ $$f(t) = (t-1)u(t-1) - 2(t-2)u(t-2) + (t-3)u(t-3)$$

Taking Laplace transform using second shifting property $\mathcal{L}\{(t-a)u(t-a)\} = \frac{e^{-as}}{s^2}$:

$$\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = \frac{e^{-s} - 2e^{-2s} + e^{-3s}}{s^2} = \mathbf{\frac{e^{-s}(1 - e^{-s})^2}{s^2}}$$
2022 (20 Series) Q.8(b) Second-Order Initial Value Problem

Using Laplace Transformations, solve $y'' - 4y' + 4y = 64\sin 2t; y(0)=0, y'(0)=1$.
